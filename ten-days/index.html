10 Days to Management Success

One lesson a day, each focused on one skill. Remember, great managers are made, not born.

Day 1Introduction to Management

The move from individual contributor (IC) to manager is about more than a title change. It's about evolving your mindset.

  • Understand the roleA manager's success isn't just about personal output. It's about guiding and empowering your team to multiply your impact. Start by asking your manager for clear expectations on what success looks like in this role.
  • Embrace the changeMoving from doing to leading changes how you view your work and your team. If you manage a big team, you'll spend most of your time enabling the work of others rather than doing it yourself. You also have to think about how you'll develop your team and yourself from now on.
  • Set the right expectationsDevelop a clear vision of what success looks like for your team and communicate it: “If we were to knock this work out of the park, we would hit these metrics by this date with no issues along the way.” Then do the same for good performance: “When we all work together, we work at our best, so this work will require us all to keep each other updated, attend every meeting, and come prepared to share updates, risks, and needs for support.” This is the foundation of good feedback, which we'll cover on Day 6.

Remember, great managers are made, not born. You're on the right path.

Day 2Effective Communication

Clear communication is the cornerstone of successful management.

  • Be clear and conciseAvoid fuzzy language that has to be interpreted. “We all need to be more proactive” or “That deliverable just wasn't that good” leave people guessing. Use concrete behaviors instead: “I think we should spend more time actively reaching out to our internal partners instead of waiting for a reply. What do you all think?” or “The presentation was unclear because there was a lot of text on the screen at once.”
  • Listen activelyCommunication is a two-way street. Avoid distractions during 1-on-1s, take notes, stay curious, ask more questions than you think you should, and always assume positive intent.
  • Adapt your styleDifferent team members need different approaches. Some want to connect on a personal level more than others. Some want public recognition, others private.

Keep practicing these skills and you'll see a significant difference in your team's dynamics.

Day 3Time Management

As a manager, your time management affects your team as much as it affects you.

  • Identify prioritiesLearn to tell urgent tasks from important ones.
  • Delegate effectivelyTrust your team with tasks to manage your workload better. Empower them to accomplish what you want without telling them exactly how to do it.
  • Use your priorities to manage workloadClear priorities show you how to invest your limited time across multiple responsibilities. They also give you the language to push back when something else is more urgent and important.

Good time management is about working smarter, not harder.

Day 4Team Motivation

Understanding what drives each team member is key.

  • Discover intrinsic motivatorsEach person is motivated differently. Do they want to advance in their career or stay where they are? Why do they work, and who do they work for? How do they like to be rewarded and recognized?
  • Set achievable goalsPeople like to push themselves, but not when what they're asked to do feels unattainable or unfair.
  • Celebrate successesSmall wins keep motivation high. Build a culture of celebrating each other's successes.

Motivated teams can achieve incredible results.

Day 5Conflict Resolution

Conflict is natural in any team. Today is about handling it effectively.

  • Stay neutralApproach conflicts without bias. Think about times your manager felt unfair to you. How can you avoid similar moments?
  • Encourage open communicationLet team members express their concerns. Ask about issues during regular 1-on-1s, then listen actively and ask questions when they open up.
  • Seek win-win solutionsAim for resolutions that satisfy everyone involved. The best solutions leave both people feeling they resolved the conflict together.

Handled well, conflict can lead to growth and better understanding. Address things early and head on, before they fester and damage your team culture.

Read more: Conflict management skills for every manager

Day 6Giving Effective Constructive Feedback

Giving feedback is a critical skill for any manager. Here's how to do it right.

  • Be specificVague feedback helps no one. List behaviors you've observed. No one is simply lazy or unmotivated, but perhaps they were late to three meetings in a row or missed several critical deadlines.
  • Focus on growthShare up front that your intent is to help them develop. It frames the conversation in a positive light.
  • Create a safe environmentMake sure people feel supported, not judged. This should be a two-way conversation, so invite their perspective. You may not have the full picture.

Feedback is a tool for growth. Use it often, and balance positive and constructive feedback.

Day 7Goal Setting

Your team's goals should reflect the broader company vision.

  • Understand company visionYou often have a broader view of how your team's work connects to the company's mission. Make that connection crystal clear, especially for new team members. People like to know their work matters.
  • Use SMART goalsSet goals that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.
  • Involve your teamTaking part in goal setting can boost commitment and performance. Let team members collaborate on specific metrics, even if they can't choose the projects they work on.

Aligned goals lead to unified effort and success. Once they're set, track progress regularly so you can get ahead of any risks.

Day 8Emotional Intelligence

Emotional intelligence (EQ) is a key component of effective leadership.

  • Self-awarenessBe conscious of your emotions and how they affect your decisions. Journaling what went well and what didn't at the end of each day is a great way to practice.
  • EmpathyUnderstand and respect your team members' feelings. Take their perspective and consider how a decision may affect them before you make it.
  • Regulate your responsesRespond, don't react. When you hear something difficult, take a deep breath, collect your thoughts, and let your team know you care.

Teams that feel safe admitting mistakes and asking questions regularly outperform those who don't.

Day 9Building a Positive Team Culture

A positive team culture is vital for success.

  • Promote opennessPsychological safety leads to high-performing teams, so make it safe to share ideas and feedback. Build familiarity by asking for feedback yourself and modeling how to respond to it.
  • Value inclusivityDiversity is a strength. Teams with different perspectives and ways of thinking are often more innovative. The aim with new hires shouldn't be sameness, whether of background or of skills.
  • Invest in team buildingRegular team activities strengthen bonds. Make time for your team to learn and develop together, and encourage collaboration.

A great culture leads to a more engaged and productive team.

Read more: How toxic culture drives people away, and how to avoid it

Day 10Delegation

On the final day, let's focus on delegation, a key skill for any successful manager.

  • Identify the right tasksNot everything can or should be delegated, but it's time to let go of some recurring tasks so you can focus on higher-level work.
  • Choose the right peopleDelegate to those with the appropriate skills and interest. Everyone can be delegated to, but some need more direction than others.
  • Provide clear instructions and supportDefine what needs to get done, by when, what good looks like, what's in scope and out of bounds, and why it's important.

Delegation is about empowering your team, not offloading work, so follow up regularly once it's delegated. Congratulations on finishing the course! You're now equipped with the fundamentals to thrive as a manager. We didn't have time for coaching, another fundamental skill, but you can learn it through 1-on-1 training and coaching on the topics most relevant to you. Keep going, and revisit these lessons as you grow in your role.
